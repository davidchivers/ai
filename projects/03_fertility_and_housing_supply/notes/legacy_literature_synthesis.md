# Literature Synthesis - 03_Fertility_and_Housing_Supply

Date: 2026-02-25

## Objective
Maintain an evidence-first synthesis linking housing-supply policy to fertility outcomes with clear identification credibility.

## Evidence structure
Layer 1 (upstream):
- Supply constraints / planning policy -> prices and unit composition.

Layer 2 (downstream):
- Housing costs and unit mix -> fertility timing and completed fertility.

Layer 3 (dynamic background):
- Demographic shifts and housing dynamics feedback over time.

## Key findings from reviewed material
- Strong support exists for upstream supply-to-price mechanisms.
- Mechanism-consistent fertility evidence is improving, including structural quantification work.
- Design-based reduced-form evidence directly on fertility remains thinner and should be expanded for balance.

## Working paper map (evidence role)
- Saiz (2010): supply-elasticity heterogeneity foundation.
- Hilber & Vermeulen: planning constraints and price amplification.
- Freemark (2019): reform-to-price response can precede quantity response.
- Couillard (2025): direct housing-fertility mechanism in structural framework.
- Francke & Korevaar (2022): dynamic background on demographics and housing cycles.

## Gaps to close next
1. Add more reduced-form fertility responses to plausibly exogenous housing shocks.
2. Add clearer timing decomposition (first-birth delay vs completed fertility).
3. Expand UK-specific evidence where institutional comparability is strongest.
