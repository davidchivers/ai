# 20 Top-five strategy review

Last updated: 2026-04-09
Status: upload-ready strategic review memo

## Bottom line

The best current version of the metal project is no longer the old blockbuster-shock idea. The
best live paper is:

- `local scenes -> later genre emergence`

with the project framed as an economics paper about collaborative innovation in a project-based
creative industry, using heavy metal as the empirical laboratory.

The strongest current headline is also clear:

- local spawning flow
- target-genre active bands
- target-genre multi-band musicians

all predict later `city x genre_family x year` emergence in the preferred exact-year fixed-effects
scene panel.

My read is:

- there is already a real paper here
- the current main scene paper is much better than the older shock-centered versions
- the project is closer to a strong field-journal or high-quality general-interest niche paper than
  to a genuine `top 5` paper right now
- a true `top 5` path likely needs one more leap beyond the current reduced-form scene result:
  either a sharper external validation layer, or a narrower but cleaner quasi-causal extension

## What the project has already tried

### 1. Domestic breakthrough shock as the main design

This was the original project spine: curated blockbuster albums, official chart archives, and
country-year treatment panels built from chart or certification timing.

What was achieved:

- a real multi-country treatment workflow
- official-source and manual-source treatment builds across `AUS`, `BRA`, `DEU`, `GBR`, `ITA`,
  and `USA`
- event-window and residualized influence memos
- a first `country x genre_family x year` domestic-success pilot

What the current evidence says:

- the treatment build is real
- the design is interesting
- but the pilot fixed-effects read is mixed to negative
- the treatment timing is still heterogeneous and partly late-coded
- the branch currently looks weaker than the scene paper

Best read:

- keep this as a fallback paper or later extension
- do not let it drive the main metal paper now

Main files:

- `data/processed/blockbuster_country_year_hit_summary.md`
- `data/processed/country_genre_analysis/domestic_success_pilot_fe_summary.md`
- `data/processed/country_genre_analysis/domestic_success_pilot_fe_summary_shock_clean.md`

### 2. Community-precedes-label threshold branch

This was the earlier network-history version of the question: do recognizable local communities
show up before the genre label fully stabilizes?

What was learned:

- the romantic version of this story shrank sharply under cleaner geography and stricter thresholds
- some interesting cases remain
- but the headline is no longer broad or stable enough to anchor the paper

Best read:

- useful as historical motivation or appendix mechanism material
- not strong enough as the main empirical design

### 3. Large network visual branch

The project built genuinely interesting city-genre network exports and previews, especially the
Helsinki black-metal connector-core case.

What was learned:

- some visuals are attractive
- but the full maps are fragmented
- even the cleaner trims are not yet obvious paper-quality headline figures

Best read:

- park the network figure until there is a clean paper-quality object
- do not wait on it before advancing the paper

### 4. Band-success sidecar

This branch was redesigned from vague `band success` into a much better staged upgrading ladder:

- `home_market_presence`
- `home_market_validation`
- `foreign_market_crossover`
- `validated_then_foreign`

What was learned:

- conceptually, this is much better than `ever successful`
- empirically, it is still sparse
- the median lag from founding to first home-market presence is long
- the cohort-winner counts are too small for a serious regression paper right now

Best read:

- keep it as a disciplined sidecar
- do not promote it into the main draft unless coverage improves materially

Main file:

- `notes/11_band_upgrading_ladder.md`

### 5. Diffusion branch

This branch built animated maps, band-world maps, and distance-weighted hub-exposure panels.

What was learned:

- visually, diffusion is real and interesting
- descriptively, genres spread through hub formation and later spread
- empirically, the external hub-exposure terms mostly wash out once local same-genre thickness is
  included

Best read:

- keep diffusion as descriptive support
- do not pivot into a diffusion paper with the current evidence

Main file:

- `notes/15_multi_genre_diffusion_check.md`

### 6. Digital and broadband extension

This branch asked whether the local-scene result weakens in the digital era and whether broadband
can provide a cleaner shock.

What was learned:

- the digital-era split is useful and nuanced:
  local spawning flow and band stock attenuate, but multi-band musician depth stays stable
- the current local broadband objects are too late or too small for the main paper
- the best serious broadband path would be a country-specific engineering-rollout design, not the
  current global city panel

Best read:

- keep the digital split as a descriptive robustness check
- do not claim a broadband shock in the main paper

Main files:

- `notes/16_digital_era_split_probe.md`
- `notes/19_early_broadband_shock_memo.md`

## Best current paper

The best current paper is:

- a reduced-form economics paper on local collaborative innovation and niche formation

The clean version of the one-sentence contribution is:

Heavy metal scenes emerge earlier in cities where local spinout flow is higher, the focal genre
already has thicker active project stock, and musicians are already recombining across multiple
local projects within that genre.

That is much stronger than:

- a mixed chart-shock paper
- a history-of-famous-bands paper
- a network-visualization paper

## Ranked top 5 path forward

### 1. Finish the scene paper as a narrow, disciplined paper

This is the best immediate use of the existing evidence.

What to do:

- tighten `drafts/metal.tex`
- keep the main empirical package centered on:
  - the critical-mass descriptive figure
  - the preferred mechanism figure or table
  - the compact reduced-form interpretation
- keep claims in `predicts`, `tracks`, and `is associated with` language

Why this is first:

- the main paper already exists
- every major side branch looks weaker than the scene result

### 2. Make the measurement package unusually defensible

If the paper is reduced-form rather than causal, the measurement has to look exceptionally clean.

What to do:

- keep sample construction and variable crosswalk materials tight
- make the emergence threshold and geography choices fully auditable
- show robustness to nearby threshold choices without exploding the paper
- make clear why `fifth band` is an operational threshold rather than a literal birth claim

Why this matters:

- the paper wins or loses on whether the reader believes the scene object is disciplined rather
  than arbitrary

### 3. Add one external validation layer, not five new branches

The strongest upgrade from here is likely validation, not more internal permutations.

Inference:

- the paper would get materially better if one outside source or outside coding logic supported the
  core emergence pattern

Candidate validation routes:

- a second source for scene existence or genre presence
- a manual validation packet for a selected set of emergence cases
- an alternative emergence rule showing the same ranking of cities or predictors

Why this is better than new side branches:

- it strengthens the main claim directly
- it avoids reopening the whole project design

### 4. Treat side branches as appendices, talks, or separate papers

The project has too many interesting branches. That is a strength for the research agenda, but it
is a risk for the main paper.

Recommended treatment:

- band-upgrading ladder: appendix or follow-on paper
- diffusion maps: talk or appendix support
- threshold-history cases: appendix motivation
- network figure: only if it becomes obviously paper quality
- domestic breakthrough shock: fallback or separate paper if re-dated and cleaned

Why this matters:

- the main paper becomes much better when it stops trying to explain everything at once

### 5. Only pursue the `top 5` leap as a second-stage extension

The current paper does not need a `top 5` leap to become publishable. But if the goal is genuine
upside rather than just a strong paper, the next leap probably has to be one of these:

- a cleaner quasi-causal extension inside a narrower country or institutional setting
- a validation layer that makes the scene-emergence object unusually convincing
- a stronger bridge from the scene result to a broader economics literature on innovation,
  agglomeration, and recombination

My ranking:

- best near-term paper strategy: finish the reduced-form scene paper
- best medium-term upside strategy: build one clean extension after the scene paper is stable
- worst strategy: keep broadening the project before the core paper is finished

## What would make it as good as possible

### What to emphasize

- this is about local innovation systems in creative industries
- metal is the empirical laboratory, not the niche audience
- the key mechanisms are labor pooling, spinouts, and recombination
- the scene result is interesting because it is local and project-based, not because it is metal

### What to cut or downweight

- generic network-science language
- causal overclaiming
- too much broker or connector rhetoric in the headline
- too much space on the shock branch in the main draft
- too many different main figures

### What the final main paper should probably look like

- one clean question
- one transparent outcome
- one main table
- two or three figures at most
- one compact conceptual framework
- one appendix that makes the data construction impossible to dismiss as hand-wavy

## Hard truth on `top 5`

My honest read is:

- the current project is good
- the current strategy is finally mostly right
- but `top 5` is not a label you get just by adding more material

The current best route to a very strong paper is:

1. finish the scene paper cleanly
2. make the measurement and appendix package unusually tight
3. then decide whether one high-upside extension can raise the ceiling

If you try to force the `top 5` leap before the core paper is settled, the more likely outcome is a
less coherent paper rather than a better one.
