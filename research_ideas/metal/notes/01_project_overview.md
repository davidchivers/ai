# 01 Project overview

Last updated: 2026-03-21
Status: living document (update in place)

## Project intention
This project studies whether blockbuster metal albums generate later local band formation. The
baseline design treats a visible album breakthrough as a shock to salience, legitimacy, and
perceived feasibility in a country's metal scene, then asks whether more bands form afterward.

The project is about creative entry after visible success. It can reuse some outcome-side data
infrastructure from earlier work, but the mechanism and empirical design here are distinct enough
to need their own notes and treatment build.

## Motivating intuition
The motivating example is a band like Sepultura. The relevant question is not only whether later
bands copied Sepultura's exact sound. The deeper question is whether a Brazilian metal breakthrough
made starting a metal band feel more feasible, more visible, and more socially legible in Brazil.

That logic can operate through several channels at once. A breakthrough can reveal market demand,
make local success feel attainable, attract labels and promoters, increase venue attention, and
thicken peer networks. The effect therefore does not have to stay inside one narrow style. A band
can matter for later metal entry even if many downstream bands do not sound much like it.

## Central research question
Do blockbuster metal albums create later entry waves in the same country, or in countries that
share the same language and cultural market?

## What the project is really about
The project is about cultural entrepreneurship and startup catalysts in creative production. The
main object is downstream entry, not stylistic influence by itself.

That is why the secondary live question is also important:

Which bands appear most influential in generating later startup waves once country and year
structure are taken into account?

Here, "influential" means associated with later band formation after a breakthrough event. That
influence could show up in:

- later all-metal startups
- later startups in the same broad genre family
- later unsigned startups
- later signed or more institutionalized startups

## Journal fit and ambition
Current read:

- Top 5 economics: no, not as currently framed.
- Top general-interest field economics: no, not yet.
- Strong field-journal economics paper: maybe, if the design becomes much sharper and the framing
  clearly generalizes beyond metal.
- Best current fit as it stands: interdisciplinary work in cultural economics, economic geography,
  or sociology of music and genres.

Why the current bar is lower:

- the project still looks partly like a clever music-domain application rather than a broadly
  important economics question
- the treatment timing remains noisy and partly manual
- the current evidence is still descriptive rather than close to causal
- the external relevance beyond metal is asserted more than demonstrated

What would move it up a tier:

- make the object explicitly broader than metal: domestic breakthrough and later producer entry in
  music scenes, with metal as the first lab rather than the whole contribution
- lock a credible `country x genre x year` breakthrough design with strong within-genre
  comparisons
- show that domestic success matters more than foreign success and that same-language foreign
  spillovers sit in between those two margins
- recover cleaner, more auditable breakthrough timing across several genres or markets
- show that the mechanism is about entrepreneurial entry under uncertainty, not just subcultural
  imitation
- make the contribution legible to economists who do not care about metal

The practical implication is that this idea should be treated as promising but not yet paper-ready.
It is worth continuing if the sharper country-genre design works. If that design stalls, the idea
probably stays below the bar for a strong economics outlet.

The strongest high-end version is therefore:

- domestic success as the main treatment
- same-language foreign success as the first spillover margin
- other foreign success as the weaker comparison margin

That is the version most likely to become a broad paper about entrepreneurial entry, cultural
market boundaries, and belief updating rather than a niche paper about metal history.

## Scope
### In scope
- all-metal band entry from the Metallum snapshot as the baseline outcome
- country-year breakthrough exposure as the baseline treatment object
- later extension to language-market spillovers
- later heterogeneity on unsigned entry, genre-family entry, and band influence ranking

### Out of scope in phase 1
- exact global sales-by-country panels
- narrow technical-death-metal outcome definitions
- global all-country treatment coverage before the first core-country workflow is stable
- strong causal claims before the treatment measurement is credible

## Working hypotheses
- H1: Home-market blockbuster breakthroughs increase later local metal entry.
- H2: Country effects are stronger for home-market breakthroughs than for globally famous foreign
  bands without the same local identity signal.
- H3: Some bands mostly affect unsigned or garage-stage entry, while others matter more for later
  signed or institutionalized entry.
- H4: Language spillovers matter, but they should appear only after the country-year hit panel is
  working.

## Alternative mechanism to test
The current evidence makes a second story live enough that it should be treated as a real
alternative rather than as a fallback excuse. The alternative is that local scene complements help
predict domestic breakout success.

On that reading, a breakout band is partly an outcome of prior scene thickening rather than a clean
shock that starts the process. The relevant complements include:

- prior local band density in the same broad genre family
- unsigned entry depth
- labels, venues, promoters, and studios
- local know-how and peer networks
- audience depth and scene visibility

That implies a different sequence:

- scene complements accumulate
- breakout success becomes more likely
- breakout success may then validate or amplify the scene further

This alternative fits the current pre-trend problem better than a pure one-way shock story. It
does not kill the original project, but it does mean the project should explicitly test whether
breakout bands are scene milestones rather than scene starters.

## Contribution framing
The economic contribution is not "music influences music" in a loose sense. The stronger framing
is that visible success stories lower uncertainty for would-be creators and help organize local
scene formation. If the data cooperate, the paper would speak to cultural entrepreneurship,
information frictions, and the geography of creative entry.

The empirical contribution is also unusual. Instead of studying listening, sales, or streaming
alone, the project studies production entry: how many new bands appear after visible success
shocks in the same cultural market.

## Main risks
The main risk is identification. Strong scenes may generate hits, and hits may then reinforce
strong scenes. So the first pass should be honest about being a reduced-form or event-style design
until cleaner shock variation is established.

The second risk is treatment measurement. The outcome side is already usable. The hard part is
recovering album success by country and year from auditable sources without turning the project
into an endless manual data-collection exercise.

The third risk is mechanism misclassification. If scene complements predict breakout success, then
the wrong empirical design is a simple post-breakthrough level shift in band counts. In that case
the project needs to test prediction, acceleration, or scene-thickening margins rather than only a
naive causal jump after the coded event year.

## Design lock questions
1. How quickly should the project complement the baseline country-year chart-hit panel with a
   cleaner first-hit event-study file for interpretation?
2. Should the first influence measure stay anchored on all-metal entry, or should unsigned entry be elevated
   earlier as a more direct entrepreneurial margin?
3. How much weight should be placed on language spillovers before the country-year treatment panel
   is stable?
4. How concentrated should the curated seed be in global anchors versus home-market flagships?

## Near-term roadmap
### Near-term
- Keep the new Brazil supplement explicitly provisional while using it to test the domestic
  exemplar mechanism.
- Keep expanding the home-market side of the current event panel, not just pooled foreign hits.
- Decide whether the domestic side should stay anchored on top-10 timing only or add a secondary
  official chart-presence margin for markets like Italy.

### Mid-term
- Build a cleaner multi-market country-year hit panel from official chart and certification data,
  with Brazil either hardened or replaced by cleaner source markets where needed.
- Keep the home-market versus foreign-market coding in the same baseline workflow.
- Test whether the same event panel can support all-metal, genre-family, and unsigned-entry
  outcomes.

### Later
- Add shared-language spillovers if the country-year design shows enough signal.
- Expand beyond the first core markets if the treatment collection workflow is scalable.
- Revisit more exact sales measures only if an auditable path becomes available.
