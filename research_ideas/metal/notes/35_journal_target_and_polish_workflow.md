# Journal target and polish workflow

Date: 2026-05-14

## Current submission posture

The main metal paper should be treated as a strong field-journal paper, not a top-five or AER-style
paper. The defensible contribution is a new setting and dataset for studying local creative
production, with a disciplined reduced-form result on local scene thickness, overlapping workers,
and genre-emergence timing.

The experienced-arrival branch should remain separate for now. It is the best causal follow-on,
but it has not yet earned main-paper status.

## Target ranking

### First target: Journal of Cultural Economics

This is the cleanest topical fit. The journal explicitly covers economic analysis of creative and
performing arts, heritage, and cultural industries, including economic organization, producers,
consumers, policy, theory, and econometric applications.

Why it fits:

- the paper is about cultural production and creative-industry organization;
- the data are unusual and field-specific, which is an asset rather than a liability here;
- the reduced-form design is less likely to be fatal if the contribution is framed as measurement,
  setting, and mechanism evidence rather than causal identification.

What to polish for this target:

- make the contribution read as economics of cultural production, not just economic geography;
- explain why metal is a useful creative-industry laboratory;
- make the measurement validation and historical sanity checks more visible;
- keep the causal language disciplined.

### Second target: Industrial and Corporate Change

This is the strongest innovation and industry-evolution target if the framing is shifted from
music economics to creative-industry evolution. The journal describes its scope around corporate
organization, innovation, industrial structures and dynamics, and evidence on industrial change,
with room for economics, management, history, sociology, and political science.

Why it fits:

- the paper has an industry-evolution object: new genre categories emerge from local organizational
  variety and overlapping project labor;
- the model language around spinouts, recombination, and local labor pooling maps naturally into
  evolutionary and organizational innovation themes;
- the setting is unusual but not frivolous if written as a project-based creative industry.

What to polish for this target:

- strengthen the industry-evolution contribution in the introduction;
- add more historical/institutional description of how metal scenes and subgenres form;
- keep the theory section concrete and mechanism-forward;
- avoid sounding like a narrow music fandom paper.

### Ambitious geography route: Journal of Economic Geography or Economic Geography

This route is plausible but higher risk. The paper would need to lean hard into local knowledge,
clusters, spatial organization of creative labor, and the geography of innovation. The economics
must be readable to geographers, and the paper should avoid overclaiming causal identification.

Why it might fit:

- the paper is fundamentally about local scene formation and spatial concentration of creative
  production;
- the data speak directly to local clusters, worker overlap, and category formation;
- the setting can contribute to economic geography debates on clusters, tacit knowledge, and
  local context.

Main risk:

- these journals may expect a broader geography conversation and stronger conceptual framing than
  the current short economics-style draft gives them.

### Not first: Regional Science and Urban Economics

This is a respected spatial-economics journal, but it is not the first target for the current
draft. Its scope includes empirical papers with a spatial dimension, but its current aims and scope
state that empirical papers studying causal mechanisms are expected to propose a convincing
identification strategy. The current main paper is intentionally reduced-form.

Use this route only if:

- the arrival sidecar becomes a cleaned causal design; or
- the main paper is reframed as a clearly non-causal spatial measurement and reduced-form paper,
  with no implied mechanism identification.

### Stretch only: Research Policy

Research Policy is relevant to innovation and technology broadly, but the current main paper is not
yet strong enough for that route. It would need a sharper innovation-policy or innovation-systems
contribution, and probably the sidecar would need to survive as a credible causal human-capital
arrival design.

## Polish workflow

The next writing pass should not add random length. It should add reader-facing substance where the
draft is currently too compressed.

1. Choose `Journal of Cultural Economics` as the default target voice for the next draft.
2. Add a stronger setting/data section:
   - what Metal Archives records;
   - how city, genre, and membership histories are constructed;
   - why genre emergence is a meaningful cultural-production outcome;
   - why the archive is useful despite not being a literal census.
3. Add one short institutional subsection on how metal scenes and subgenres form:
   - bands as projects;
   - members moving across projects;
   - local scenes as labor pools;
   - genre labels as retrospective but operationally meaningful categories.
4. Tighten the introduction around a single contribution:
   - local project labor and overlapping memberships predict transitions into operational scene
     status in a creative industry.
5. Keep the arrival branch out of the main text except as future-work language unless the clean
   `98`-event stack survives manual event audit.

## Working decision

Default target: `Journal of Cultural Economics`.

Higher-risk alternative: `Industrial and Corporate Change`.

Do not aim first at `Regional Science and Urban Economics` unless the paper gains a cleaner causal
design.
