# AER-style referee report, round 5

Paper: `Local scene emergence in heavy metal`

Date: 2026-04-10

## Recommendation

Reject

## Summary assessment

This is now a strong and disciplined version of the paper on its own terms. The manuscript has a
clear empirical object, a transparent outcome, a large and unusual data construction, and a much
more honest statement of what the design can and cannot support. The threshold-response and
measurement-validation packages are serious improvements, and the recent compression of the
front-end sections helps the paper read more like one bounded economics paper rather than a bundle
of adjacent ideas.

My recommendation at the AER standard nevertheless remains rejection. The central reason is still
the same. The preferred exact-year fixed-effects specification identifies a robust within-cell
transition pattern, but not a causal account of scene formation. The paper's strongest evidence is
that city-genre cells with more local spawning, thicker focal-genre band stock, and deeper
within-genre overlapping-worker pools are more likely to cross an operational scene threshold in
the near future. That is a good reduced-form result. It is not, in my view, an AER contribution.

## Confidential comments to the editor

The manuscript has improved substantially over the revision cycle. The author has responded in the
right way to earlier criticism: by narrowing the claim, cutting distracting side branches,
validating the measurement object, and clarifying that the paper is strongest on late-stage local
niche consolidation rather than the earliest seed stage of scene formation. This is now a coherent
and respectable field-paper draft.

But the core outlet mismatch remains. The paper's empirical design is still built around an
operational threshold-crossing event, and the main regressors remain tightly connected to the same
local buildup process that mechanically governs approach to that threshold. City-genre fixed
effects and year effects substantially improve discipline, but they do not create exogenous
variation in local spawning, focal stock, or multi-band depth. The paper is therefore best read as
a well-validated within-cell hazard result rather than a design that identifies how new creative
scenes are causally generated.

If the editor's standard is whether the manuscript is now a serious economics paper, my answer is
yes. If the standard is whether it clears the AER bar on contribution and identification, my
answer remains no.

## Comments to the author

This draft is markedly better than the earlier versions. The recent revisions improved the paper in
the correct margins rather than adding more scope. The result is now understandable, bounded, and
defensible. My practical advice is the same as in the prior round, but now with more confidence:
stop trying to make this an AER paper. It is close to a good field-journal paper, and the marginal
gains from further work are now more likely to come from restraint than from expansion.

## Major concerns

### 1. The central result is still not a causal account of scene formation

The key unresolved issue is not whether the result is interesting. It is whether the design can
support the paper's most ambitious interpretation. The answer remains no. The preferred
specification is a within-cell predictive hazard with lagged regressors, city-genre fixed effects,
and year effects. That is much better than a cross-sectional comparison of famous and obscure
scenes, but it still does not identify the causal effect of local spawning, focal-niche stock, or
within-niche overlapping-worker depth.

This matters because two of the headline regressors are very close to the state variable that
governs approach to emergence. The paper's strongest result can still be summarized as: cells that
are already further along the local buildup process are more likely to hit the viability threshold
soon afterward. That is a respectable empirical fact. It is not a causal account of how scenes are
born.

### 2. The threshold-response package improves the paper, but also narrows it decisively

The threshold-response work is useful and worth keeping. It shows that the result is not pinned to
one exact cutoff. But the more demanding exercises point in a sobering direction: once the sample
is restricted to cells still far below the threshold, the pattern weakens materially and the
multi-band term is no longer clearly estimated. This means the paper's validated contribution is
not a general theory of scene origination from thin initial conditions. It is a paper about the
later transition from scattered local activity to viable local scene status.

The draft is now mostly honest about this, which is an improvement. But that narrowing also
confirms why the paper remains below the AER bar.

### 3. The mechanism interpretation is disciplined, but still sits slightly ahead of what is identified

The manuscript now uses better language than before, and that is to its credit. Still, the
mechanism story should be read as economically informed interpretation layered onto a reduced-form
finding, not as identified mechanism. Spawning is a proxy for embedded entry, not direct evidence
of knowledge transfer. Multi-band depth is consistent with recombination, not identified
recombination. The paper is strongest when it states that these margins track later emergence
rather than claiming that it has isolated the causal channels.

### 4. The contribution is now clear, but narrower than the setting's richness

The data construction is genuinely impressive. Heavy metal is an unusually good laboratory because
project overlap and worker mobility are visible in a way they often are not elsewhere. But the
economic payoff remains narrower than the richness of the setting might initially suggest. The
paper's best general statement is that localized capability, embedded project formation, and
within-niche overlapping-worker depth are associated with later local scene emergence in a
project-based creative industry. That is publishable. It is not AER-scale.

## What is working now

### 1. The paper now has a clear and coherent empirical object

The manuscript clearly defines what counts as emergence, why the rule is operational rather than
ontological, and what the outcome is intended to capture. That clarity is a major strength.

### 2. The validation work is serious

The threshold-response and measurement-validation packages materially improve credibility. They do
not solve the design's ultimate identification problem, but they do show that the result is not
simply a fragile coding artifact.

### 3. The paper is much better disciplined than before

The front end is tighter, the side branches are out of the way, and the empirical-motivation
section is now more clearly just setup. The manuscript feels centered on one result.

### 4. The paper now has a credible field-journal interpretation

The most successful conceptual move in the revision cycle is the narrowing move. The paper no
longer tries to claim more than the design can support. That makes it a better paper, even if it
does not change the outlet verdict.

## Minor comments

1. I would continue to avoid any sentence that makes the fifth-band rule sound structural rather
than operational.
2. I would keep the role-composition discussion extremely short or fully backgrounded.
3. If there is one further presentational tweak to consider, it is to make the fixed-effects result
read even more explicitly as a within-cell transition equation rather than as a quasi-structural
production function.
4. I would resist adding any further robustness material to the main text unless it directly
changes the interpretation of the preferred specification.

## Bottom line

My AER recommendation remains rejection. But this is no longer because the manuscript feels loose
or underdeveloped. It is because the final, sharpened version of the paper has clarified its true
contribution: a strong, validated, reduced-form result about local scene emergence near the later
approach to viability. That is a real contribution, and now a fairly clean one. It is simply not
an AER contribution.
