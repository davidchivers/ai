# AER-style referee report, round 3

Paper: `Local scene emergence in heavy metal`

Date: 2026-04-09

## Recommendation

Reject

## Summary assessment

This draft is again better than the previous one. The paper is now materially tighter. The
threshold-response package exists, the measurement-validation package exists, the internet and
broadband branch has been pushed out of the main narrative, and the preferred table now explains
its own scale and control structure more clearly. The manuscript now reads much more like one paper
and much less like an organized bundle of adjacent probes.

Even so, my recommendation at the AER standard remains rejection. The central issue is still that
the paper's best result is a carefully bounded reduced-form pattern rather than a design that
cleanly separates economic mechanism from late-stage threshold approach. What has changed in this
round is not the verdict, but the outlet read. The manuscript now looks closer to a serious
field-journal paper than to a sprawling working draft.

## Confidential comments to the editor

The author has revised in the right sequence. The paper is much clearer about what survives and
what does not. In particular, the draft now concedes that the strongest result is about late-stage
local niche consolidation rather than the earliest seed stage of scene formation, and it validates
the measurement system better than before. Those are real gains.

But the core contribution remains too reduced-form and too closely tied to an operational
threshold-crossing design for the AER. My recommendation therefore remains rejection at that
standard. My practical read, however, is more favorable than in the previous two rounds: the paper
is now close to the kind of sharply framed, well-defended empirical contribution that could travel
in a strong field outlet.

## Comments to the author

This is now a much better paper than the draft I saw at the start of the revision cycle. The
improvements are cumulative rather than cosmetic. The manuscript has become more disciplined,
better validated, and easier to read as an economics paper.

The main remaining issue is not confusion, but fit. The paper is now fairly honest about what it
does. What it does is interesting. It is just still not an AER paper. The clearest way forward is
to keep revising toward a strong field-journal submission rather than trying to stretch the design
into something it is not.

## What improved relative to round 2

### 1. The paper is now genuinely more focused

This is the most important improvement. The internet-adoption and city-broadband material no longer
dominates the main results section. The paper now stays centered on:

1. the critical-mass descriptive fact,
2. the preferred exact-year result,
3. the threshold-response package, and
4. the measurement-validation package.

That is much better paper discipline.

### 2. The manuscript now does more to validate the emergence object

The new validation package is useful. The paper now shows:

1. robustness to alternative emergence cutoffs,
2. restricted-risk-set attenuation,
3. geography-clean robustness,
4. high-coverage-country robustness,
5. and a short set of historical sanity cases.

This does not solve the identification problem, but it does make the empirical object more
defensible and more transparent.

### 3. The main table is more interpretable

The new explanation of coefficient scale is helpful. Translating the standardized coefficients into
percentage-point terms relative to the event rate makes the table feel like evidence rather than
like output. The explanation of why overall multi-band musicians enter negatively while
target-genre multi-band depth enters positively also helps materially.

## Main remaining concerns

### 1. The paper is still strongest only at the late-stage approach to emergence

The threshold-response package improves honesty, but it also narrows the contribution. The draft
now effectively shows that local spawning flow, focal-niche band stock, and focal-niche
multi-band depth predict the later approach to an operational scene threshold. That is a coherent
result. But it is not the same as showing how genuinely new scenes are born from very thin initial
conditions.

This is the main reason the AER claim still does not land. The paper now has a good bounded
result, but the bounded result is not ambitious enough for the outlet.

### 2. The manuscript still leans slightly past its best claim in places

The draft is much better than before, but there is still occasional slippage from "predicts later
emergence" toward "reveals local spinouts, labor pooling, and recombination." The latter language
is plausible and often reasonable, but the design still does not isolate those channels. The paper
should continue to prefer disciplined language such as:

1. tracks,
2. predicts,
3. is consistent with,
4. and is most naturally read as.

The more the paper speaks this way, the stronger it becomes.

### 3. Some residual side material could still be compressed

The role-composition paragraph is no longer a major problem, but it still reads as one paragraph of
additional mechanism exploration after the paper has already reached its main result. I do not view
this as fatal. But if the author wants an even tighter draft, this is the next obvious candidate
for compression or appendix relocation.

### 4. The paper is now closer to field-journal fit than to AER fit

This is no longer just a negative point. It is now also a positive one. The project has become a
fairly clear field-paper contribution:

1. novel collaboration and scene-emergence data,
2. a defendable reduced-form result,
3. serious validation of the measurement object,
4. and a restrained economics interpretation.

That is a good outcome. But it is a different outcome from the one implied by the AER target.

## Minor comments

1. The introduction is substantially improved, but the literature review could still be shortened a
bit relative to the empirical core.
2. The contributions paragraph in the introduction should continue to avoid any implication that the
paper has identified causal scene-formation mechanisms.
3. If the draft is revised again, I would consider shortening the role-composition paragraph or
moving part of it to the appendix.
4. The paper now uses its appendix well. Any further exploratory branches should go there by
default unless they directly change the interpretation of the core result.

## Bottom line

This revision cycle has clearly improved the paper. My recommendation at the AER standard remains
rejection, but my practical evaluation of the project is now more positive than before. The draft
has become a coherent, bounded, field-journal paper about local scene emergence in a project-based
creative industry.

The best next move is not to widen the design again. It is to keep narrowing and polishing the
paper around the contribution it actually has.
