# Field-journal positioning memo

Paper: `Local scene emergence in heavy metal`

Date: 2026-04-09

## Core contribution

This paper studies local scene emergence in a project-based creative industry. Using the full
Metal Archives band census and member dump, it builds a city-level collaboration dataset with
directly observed overlapping personnel ties and links that object to a dynamic `city x genre`
panel of local scene emergence.

The paper's empirical contribution is a carefully validated reduced-form result. In the preferred
exact-year fixed-effects design, later emergence tracks three stable lagged predictors:

1. local spawning flow,
2. target-genre active bands, and
3. target-genre multi-band musician depth.

That pattern survives nearby threshold changes and geography-clean validation checks, but
attenuates sharply once the risk set is restricted to cells still far below the emergence
threshold. The strongest defensible interpretation is therefore late-stage local niche
consolidation rather than the earliest seed stage of scene formation.

## Best paper claim

The paper should claim that local scene emergence is more likely in places where local capability
is already thicker, embedded project formation is more active, and overlapping workers are already
recombining within the focal niche.

The paper should not claim:

1. causal identification of scene-formation mechanisms,
2. the true historical birth date of a genre,
3. a clean estimate of knowledge spillovers,
4. or a general digital or broadband mechanism.

The right language is:

1. predicts,
2. tracks,
3. is associated with,
4. is consistent with,
5. and is most naturally read as.

## Why the paper is publishable in a field outlet

The paper now has four features that together make it a credible field-paper contribution:

1. a novel and unusually rich collaboration dataset,
2. a transparent and validated panel measure of local scene emergence,
3. a clear and bounded reduced-form result,
4. and an economics interpretation grounded in agglomeration, spinouts, and recombination rather
   than in retrospective scene history alone.

Heavy metal is a useful laboratory because project overlap and worker mobility are unusually
observable in this setting. The paper does not need to prove that the result is universal across
all creative industries. It only needs to show that this setting reveals a clear and economically
interpretable local production pattern.

## Residual risks

The main residual risks are now narrower than before:

1. the result remains tightly linked to an operational threshold-crossing outcome,
2. the paper is strongest near the later approach to emergence rather than from very thin initial
   conditions,
3. the setting remains one archival system and one cultural industry,
4. and the interpretation must remain disciplined to avoid slipping past the design.

These are real limitations, but they are compatible with a strong field-journal paper if the draft
continues to respect them.

## Practical positioning

The paper should be presented as:

1. an economics paper about localized capability and project-based creative production,
2. using heavy metal as the empirical laboratory,
3. with a validated reduced-form contribution,
4. and with a bounded claim about local scene emergence.

It should not be presented as:

1. a causal identification paper,
2. a diffusion paper,
3. a broadband paper,
4. or a general theory of how all new creative categories are born.

## Recommendation for the next draft stage

Treat the draft as close to a stable field-paper version. Further revisions should be compressive
rather than expansive:

1. keep side branches in the appendix by default,
2. preserve disciplined claim language,
3. and only reopen empirical branches if they directly strengthen the bounded main result.
