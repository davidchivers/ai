# AER-style referee report, round 4

Paper: `Local scene emergence in heavy metal`

Date: 2026-04-09

## Recommendation

Reject

## Summary assessment

This is the strongest version of the draft so far. The manuscript is now disciplined about its
object, explicit about its limits, and much better organized around one empirical contribution. The
paper introduces an unusually rich city-level collaboration dataset, constructs a transparent panel
measure of local scene emergence, and shows a stable reduced-form association between later
emergence and three lagged local conditions: spawning flow, focal-genre active band stock, and
focal-genre multi-band musician depth. The threshold-response and measurement-validation packages
materially improve the paper.

My recommendation at the AER standard nevertheless remains rejection. The central reason is now
clearer than before. The paper's strongest result is a well-validated pattern about the later
approach to an operational emergence threshold, not a design that cleanly identifies the origin of
new scenes or the causal mechanisms that generate them. That is a respectable and publishable
field-paper contribution. It is not, in my view, an AER contribution.

## Confidential comments to the editor

The paper has improved meaningfully across rounds. The author has cut distracting side branches,
disciplined the claims, added direct responses to the threshold objection, and validated the
measurement object far better than is typical for a project at this stage. I now view the paper as
coherent, honest, and substantially stronger than the earlier drafts.

But the outlet mismatch remains decisive. The paper's central empirical object is still an
operational threshold-crossing event, and the strongest evidence is still concentrated near the
later run-up to that threshold. The author is commendably clear about this now, but the clarity
does not change the underlying contribution. The paper does not produce an exogenous design, and
it does not convincingly move from "predicts crossing a local-scene threshold" to "identifies how
new creative scenes are generated." I therefore recommend rejection at the AER bar.

If the editor's question is whether the manuscript has become a credible economics paper, the
answer is yes. If the question is whether it reaches AER significance and identification standards,
my answer remains no.

## Comments to the author

This is now a much more successful paper on its own terms. The draft no longer feels like an
exploratory memo. It reads like a bounded empirical paper with a real dataset, a transparent
outcome, and a defensible reduced-form result. That is a real achievement.

The practical implication of my report is not "start over." It is "stop trying to make this an AER
paper." The manuscript is now close to a good field-journal paper. The best next moves are
compressive and positioning-oriented, not expansive.

## What is working now

### 1. The paper has a clear empirical object

The paper now states plainly what counts as emergence, why that rule is operational rather than
ontological, and what is and is not learned from that measure. This is a substantial improvement
over earlier versions.

### 2. The validation work is serious and useful

The threshold-response package and the measurement-validation package both matter. They do not
solve the paper's ultimate identification problem, but they do establish that the main result is
not a trivial artifact of one fragile coding choice.

### 3. The paper now has a credible bounded interpretation

The best current line in the manuscript is the narrowing move: the paper is strongest on late-stage
local niche consolidation rather than on the earliest seed stage of scene formation. That
interpretation is honest, defensible, and much stronger than a larger claim the design cannot
carry.

### 4. The draft is now much better disciplined

Pushing the internet and broadband material out of the core narrative was the right choice. The
main table is now interpretable. The front end is tighter. The paper now feels centered on one
result.

## Main remaining concerns

### 1. The paper still does not clear the AER bar on the central question

The core contribution remains narrower than the paper's most ambitious framing. Even after the new
packages, the manuscript still shows that thicker focal scenes with more local spawning and more
within-niche overlapping workers are more likely to cross an operational scene threshold soon
afterward. That is interesting, but it remains closer to a disciplined threshold-crossing result
than to a general account of how new scenes are born.

This remains the decisive issue for AER fit.

### 2. The mechanism language is improved but still slightly ahead of the design

The paper is now much better at using words like "tracks," "predicts," and "is consistent with."
It should stay there. Spawning flow is still a proxy for embedded entry rather than a direct
measure of knowledge transfer. Multi-band depth is still consistent with recombination rather than
identified recombination. The draft is strongest when it treats those as economically informed
interpretations layered onto a reduced-form fact.

### 3. The empirical motivation section may still be one figure too long

This is now the next obvious compression margin. The paper's empirical core is already doing most
of the work. The combination of the global proliferation figure, the subgenre tree, and the black
metal diffusion snapshots may still be more motivation than the final paper needs. I would
seriously consider trimming one of those figures, most likely the subgenre tree or moving it to
the appendix.

### 4. The general economics payoff is still narrower than the setting-specific richness

Heavy metal is a very good data laboratory because it makes worker overlap and project mobility
visible. But the broader economic lesson is still somewhat narrower than the richness of the
setting might initially suggest. The paper should keep stating the general takeaway modestly:
localized capability, embedded project formation, and within-niche recombination are associated
with later scene emergence in a project-based creative industry. That is enough.

## Minor comments

1. I would continue to keep the role-composition discussion very short or move it fully to the
appendix.
2. I would avoid any sentence that makes the fifth-band rule sound like a structural boundary
rather than an empirical coding choice.
3. If the paper is repositioned for a field journal, the abstract could be even more explicit that
the contribution is a new data object plus a validated reduced-form pattern.
4. The manuscript now uses the appendix well. Future robustness additions should go there by
default unless they directly change the interpretation of the preferred specification.

## Bottom line

My AER recommendation remains rejection. But this report is more favorable than the earlier ones in
substance, not only in tone. The manuscript now looks like a strong candidate for a field-journal
path if the author keeps the claims disciplined and resists reopening exploratory branches.

If there is one further tightening move I would make, it is to compress the empirical-motivation
section and then stop. The paper's marginal gains are now more likely to come from restraint than
from additional scope.
