# AER-style referee report, round 2

Paper: `Local scene emergence in heavy metal`

Date: 2026-04-09

## Recommendation

Reject

## Summary assessment

This revised draft is better than the previous version. The front end is materially improved. The
paper now opens with a real introduction, states the question more clearly, acknowledges the main
empirical limitation upfront, and does a better job explaining why the setting could matter for
economists. The appendix package also makes the draft feel more like a paper and less like a
working memo.

Even with those improvements, however, I do not think the paper is close to the AER bar. The main
reason is unchanged: the core empirical result is still too difficult to distinguish from a
mechanical threshold-crossing fact. The outcome is whether a `city x genre` cell reaches its fifth
band, and the key regressors remain lagged measures of local scene thickness, local spawning, and
local multi-band depth. The paper now names this problem clearly, which is good. But it does not
yet solve it.

My read is therefore similar to the first report, but more focused. The revision has removed one
important weakness, namely the front-end structure. What remains are the harder substantive issues.

## Confidential comments to the editor

The author has improved the manuscript in the right direction. The introduction is now serious,
the reduced-form contribution is stated more honestly, and the draft is more self-aware about its
main empirical risk. Those are meaningful gains.

But the central AER problem remains. The paper still does not show that it is learning much more
than the fact that thicker local activity predicts crossing a local thickness threshold. In that
sense the revision is clarifying rather than curing the main problem. The project continues to look
to me like a potentially strong field-journal paper once its claims and scope are properly aligned
with the design, but not like an AER paper.

## Comments to the author

This revision is a real improvement. The introduction is much better than before, and the paper now
states its contribution and limitations more directly. That matters. The revised draft reads more
like a submission and less like an organized working paper.

At the same time, the revision has mostly fixed the easiest problem. The hardest one remains
intact. The paper still needs a persuasive answer to the objection that the headline result is
mechanically tied to the way emergence is defined.

## What improved relative to the previous draft

### 1. The paper now has a real introduction

This is a clear improvement. The draft now states:

1. the question,
2. the data contribution,
3. the exact empirical object,
4. the main result,
5. and the main limitation.

That alone makes the paper easier to evaluate and much easier to place.

### 2. The paper is more honest about what it does not identify

The new front end is more explicit that the paper is reduced-form rather than causal and that the
emergence threshold is operational rather than ontological. This is also an improvement. The
manuscript is no longer trying to smuggle in stronger claims than the design can support.

### 3. The appendix package helps

The sample-construction and variable-definition appendices improve transparency. They do not solve
the identification issue, but they do make the empirical package more legible and more auditable.

## Main remaining concerns

### 1. The threshold-mechanics problem is still the central obstacle

This remains the main issue by a wide margin. The paper now acknowledges the problem in the
introduction, but the empirical sections still do not answer it. A reader is still left with the
basic concern that:

1. the outcome is a stock threshold,
2. one key predictor is lagged stock in the same local niche,
3. and the other key predictors are highly endogenous margins of the same local thickening process.

That is not fatal for a descriptive or predictive paper. But it is fatal for an AER-level claim
that the paper is isolating economically meaningful mechanisms in a way that materially advances
what economists know.

The next revision must bring actual evidence to this point. The paper needs:

1. threshold-robustness exercises,
2. restricted-risk-set exercises,
3. and at least one serious falsification or placebo exercise.

Without those, the current headline result remains too vulnerable.

### 2. Measurement validation is still too thin

The appendix now documents construction, but the draft still does not validate the measurement
system enough. The reader still has to accept several strong assumptions:

1. that Metal Archives behaves enough like a usable census,
2. that city parsing and genre-family assignment are not driving the result,
3. and that the fifth-band threshold tracks something substantively meaningful.

What is missing is not a longer construction table. What is missing is a validation package. I want
to see:

1. alternative thresholds,
2. geography-clean robustness,
3. country or cohort exclusions,
4. and a small set of known-scene historical sanity checks.

The paper now says this is the next task. That is the right diagnosis. But until that work is done,
the empirical object is still under-validated.

### 3. The results section remains too diffuse

The core result is actually fairly narrow, but the results section is still carrying too much
exploratory material. The digital-era split, internet-adoption event studies, and city-broadband
extension all read as side analyses that have not earned main-text space. Their current effect is
to blur the central paper rather than deepen it.

This is more damaging now that the introduction is sharper. The front end says the paper is about
localized capability, spinouts, and overlapping workers. The back end then spends several
paragraphs on internet timing evidence that is openly not cleanly causal and not clearly central to
the main result.

My advice is the same as before, but stronger now: unless these analyses are used directly to
resolve the central identification problem, move most of them to the appendix.

### 4. The general-interest economics payoff is still asserted more than shown

The new introduction is better at explaining why economists might care. But the paper still does
not convincingly show that the result travels beyond the specific institutional, archival, and
cultural features of heavy metal. The draft now makes a broad claim about localized capability and
variety formation, yet the evidence still comes from one unusual setting with one operational
threshold and one predictive design.

That can still be a good paper. But it is not yet a general-interest paper. The manuscript would
improve if it stopped leaning toward AER-style generality and instead leaned into being a sharp,
carefully defended field-paper contribution.

### 5. The main table still needs more interpretation

The preferred table is coherent, but it still leaves important questions under-explained. In
particular:

1. Why does overall multi-band depth enter negatively while target-genre multi-band depth enters
   positively?
2. How large are these effects in economically interpretable terms, not just z-score units?
3. Why should clustering by city be taken as sufficient?

These are all answerable questions, but the draft currently does not answer them clearly enough.

## Minor comments

1. The revised introduction is good, but the transition from introduction to related literature
could still be tighter. Right now the literature review remains somewhat long relative to the core
result.
2. The contribution list in the introduction is improved, but I would make the second and third
contributions even more disciplined. The draft should not imply that it has resolved scene
formation causally.
3. The results section should announce more clearly which parts are core and which are background.
4. The internet-adoption section should either move to the appendix or be rewritten much more
briefly as a supporting descriptive note.
5. The manuscript should add at least one sentence in the results section explaining why the
negative coefficient on overall multi-band musicians does not undermine the positive focal-genre
result.

## Bottom line

This is a better paper than the one I saw previously. The introduction is a real improvement, and
the manuscript now presents itself more honestly. But the revision has not yet changed my
recommendation. The main threshold-mechanics objection still dominates the paper's evaluation.

My recommendation remains rejection at the AER standard. My practical advice remains the same as
before, but with more confidence: this project should be revised toward a strong field-journal
paper centered on a carefully validated and carefully bounded reduced-form result.
