# GEM note on necessity and opportunity entrepreneurship

Status: sidecar note for intro framing and possible later motivation extensions.

Last updated: 2026-03-08

## Why GEM matters here

The paper's central measurement problem is that necessity entrepreneurship is a
latent counterfactual object. GEM is useful because it gets closer than most
public sources to observed motive data: it asks entrepreneurs why they started
their businesses and reports public aggregates on opportunity- and
necessity-related motives.

That makes GEM a good complement to the recession evidence already built in this
folder. The recession figures are useful for motivation, but they are not a
direct measure of necessity entrepreneurship. GEM provides a motive-based
object, even if it remains self-reported and therefore imperfect.

## Verified source points

### 1. Official U.S. GEM report

Verified source:
- GEM 2023--2024 United States Report, Babson College / GEM.
- Official report page: https://www.gemconsortium.org/report/gem-20232024-usa-national-report
- Official PDF endpoint: https://www.gemconsortium.org/file/open?fileId=51493

Verified facts from the report:
- The executive summary states that the biggest change in motivation for starting
  a business in 2023 came from job scarcity, with more than a one-third increase
  from 2021 to 2023.
- Figure 11 reports that 35.0 percent of entrepreneurs in the United States in
  2023 said they started a business "to earn a living because jobs are scarce."

Use for this project:
- This is strong public-facing evidence that necessity-style motives remain
  quantitatively relevant in the U.S., not only in poorer countries.
- It supports opening the paper with the identification problem rather than with
  recession facts alone.

### 2. GEM global report on cross-country variation

Verified source:
- Donna Kelley, Slavica Singer, and Mike Herrington, *Global Entrepreneurship
  Monitor 2015/16 Global Report*.
- Official report page: https://www.gemconsortium.org/report/49480
- Official PDF endpoint: https://www.gemconsortium.org/file/open?fileId=49480

Verified facts from the report:
- In factor- and efficiency-driven economies, 69 percent of entrepreneurs report
  opportunity motives rather than necessity motives.
- In innovation-driven economies, 78 percent are opportunity-motivated.
- The GEM motivational index, defined as improvement-driven opportunity relative
  to necessity entrepreneurship, is about 1.5 in factor-driven economies, 2.0 in
  efficiency-driven economies, and 3.4 in innovation-driven economies.

Use for this project:
- This is clean evidence that motive composition varies systematically across
  country groups and development stages.
- It supports the user's point that U.S. motive evidence should not be treated as
  globally representative.

### 3. GEM data access constraint

Verified source:
- GEM data access page: https://www.gemconsortium.org/data

Verified fact:
- GEM states that selected information is public, but that full APS and NES
  data become publicly available after a three-year embargo.

Use for this project:
- Public reports are enough for motivation and framing right now.
- If the paper later wants micro-level U.S. motive tabulations directly from
  GEM APS, data access may depend on the embargo window or separate access.

## Interpretation

GEM is valuable here, but it should be used carefully.

What it does well:
- it measures stated reasons for startup activity directly,
- it provides a public U.S. time-series-style motivation fact,
- it provides cross-country evidence that opportunity and necessity composition
  differs systematically with development.

What it does not solve:
- motives are self-reported,
- multiple motives can coexist,
- survey responses do not map one-for-one into the model's counterfactual
  definition of necessity entrepreneurship.

## Best use in the paper

Most defensible role:
- use GEM in the introduction and motivation to establish that necessity motives
  are real, non-trivial, and heterogeneous across countries,
- then explain why the paper moves from motive labels to a structural
  counterfactual definition.

Less defensible role:
- do not claim that GEM directly measures the model object without error,
- do not use recession evidence and GEM motives interchangeably.

## Recommended wording direction

Best opening logic:
- necessity entrepreneurship is hard to observe directly,
- survey-based evidence such as GEM is informative because it asks about motives,
- status-based evidence such as Fairlie and Fossen is informative because it
  uses observed labor-market transitions,
- both are useful but incomplete,
- the paper therefore defines necessity entrepreneurship structurally and uses
  recession evidence as supporting empirical motivation rather than as the
  definition itself.
